About this site

What it is, why these five characters, and how the thing was put together.

The idea

This is a gallery of five fictional characters I have actually spent time with, not a list of the most powerful or most popular ones. Every entry is written the way you would write a trading card: a few hard facts, a rough power rating, and one honest line about why I picked the character.

Why these five

  • Iron Man / Tony Stark — he builds himself into the role. He starts with money, ego and talent, and has to earn the hero part through suffering, responsibility and engineering.
  • Spider-Man / Peter Parker — the responsibility is handed to him. He gets real power very young and spends the whole story learning how heavy that is.
  • Jake Sully — he rejects the world he came from and chooses another one. His story is about identity, belonging and love. He does not just become a better soldier; he turns against the values of his own side.
  • Ben 10 / Ben Tennyson — the childhood fantasy. I did not watch Ben for the philosophy. I watched because a kid who can turn into ten aliens is an incredible idea, and the Omnitrix was every kid's dream.
  • Tom and Jerry — pure entertainment that does not age. Almost no dialogue, no mythology, no character arcs. The physical comedy works whether you are 5 or 50, and that is why it stuck with me.

Design choices

The design looks like a collection of cards sitting on a table. I used a dark teal background so the cream-colored cards stand out and look more like real physical cards. Each card also has a brass-colored line around the edge to make the different pages feel connected. The character names use a serif font, while the facts and labels use a sans-serif font. This makes it easier to tell the difference between a character's name and their stats.

The stats on each card are organized with the label on the left and the value on the right. This makes all five cards easy to scan and compare instead of making them look like five separate paragraphs. For the power level, I used five small squares instead of a number. Since the power rating is just a general judgment, the squares make more sense than using something exact like "8.4 / 10."

How it was built

Plain HTML and one hand-written stylesheet. No frameworks, no JavaScript, no build step. The gallery uses CSS grid with auto-fill, so it reflows from three columns down to one on its own. There is one media query at the end of the stylesheet, and it handles the leftovers: it stacks the two-column layouts on the details and about pages, and shrinks the page heading on small screens.
